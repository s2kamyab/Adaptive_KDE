# Figure captions

## fig1_fitted_densities

Fitted densities on a single dataset per panel, in original data units. Rows: four Marron-Wand targets; columns: N = 200 and N = 5000. Within a panel every method is fitted to the identical observations, namely confirmation replicate 0 (the first prespecified seed); no seed was chosen after inspecting results. Annotations give the ISE and the number of mixture components K of AKDE-V2 and AKDE-M; complete per-curve metrics are in data/fig1_fitted_densities_metrics.csv. The plotting window is the predeclared truth quantile range [0.0005, 0.9995] and was never used in fitting; the largest fitted mass outside that window over all curves is 0.0120. No y-limit cap is applied, so the AKDE-V2 spikes are shown at full height. Where a curve is not visible it coincides with another, not because it is missing.

## fig2_variability_N1000

Variability of the fitted density across 30 independent confirmation datasets at N = 1000. Each panel shows the arithmetic mean fitted density and the pointwise 10th-90th percentile band across replicates, against the ground truth. These are EMPIRICAL VARIABILITY BANDS describing the spread of the estimator across datasets: they are not confidence bands for the true density and carry no simultaneous coverage statement. The arithmetic mean of normalised densities is itself a density (measured mass 0.9984 to 0.9997 across panels); the pointwise median curve is not constrained to integrate to one (measured mass 0.8711 to 1.0004) and is therefore not plotted.

## fig3_accuracy_vs_N

Accuracy over the locked confirmation (15 targets, N in {200, 1000, 5000}, R = 30 independent datasets per cell). Top left: benchmark mean ISE giving each target equal weight, with 95% percentile bootstrap CIs obtained by resampling replicate IDs within each target-N cell (B = 10,000), carrying all methods together so pairing is preserved; the target index is never resampled because the 15 densities are a fixed benchmark. Top right: the fixed-benchmark paired effect exp(T), where T is the equal-weight mean over targets of the mean paired log ISE ratio against AKDE-V2, with the same bootstrap scheme. Middle: per-target paired ratios AKDE-M / AKDE-V2 with paired bootstrap CIs; all 15 targets are shown at all three sample sizes and none is omitted. Bottom: log10 mean ISE for every method and every target. Any apparent trend in N describes the observed range only; three sample sizes cannot support a convergence-rate claim.

## fig4_mechanism

Mechanism diagnostics, all from the frozen 16-dataset development subset. (a) Oracle ladder: the exact risk is split into the dictionary optimum, the excess incurred by the moment criterion when sampling error is removed by substituting population moments and the population covariance, and the further gap on moving to empirical quantities. Left bar of each pair is the V2 measurement bank, right bar the matched bank; log scale. The population control removes sampling error IN THE MOMENTS conditional on a dictionary that is still built from the sample; it does not remove approximation, constraint, weighting or numerical effects, so the middle band is not criterion bias alone. (b) Measured moment discrepancy against fitted density error: AKDE-V2 satisfies its own moment conditions to within their sampling noise (dotted line at two standard errors) while leaving a large shape error. (c) Largest ISE change achieved by a feasible perturbation of the exact-risk optimum within a stated relative weighted-moment budget, searching the full feasible tangent cone {v : 1'v = 0, v_i >= 0 where w_i = 0}. These are WITNESSES, i.e. lower bounds; no global upper bound on ambiguity is claimed. (d) Stage-1 optimisation accuracy: relative KKT residual for coordinate descent at three iteration budgets and for an independent proximal-gradient solver. Neither reliably reaches the 1e-8 target.

## fig5_cost_and_compactness

Cost and compactness. (a) mean ISE against the mean number of mixture components and (b) against the mean cold end-to-end fit time, one point per (target, N) cell. (c) density-evaluation latency, measured on the SAVED fitted models with identical query points (10,000 per model, batch 2,000), identical warmup and 7 timing repeats per model, in the same process on the same hardware with BLAS pinned to one thread; the reported value is the median over repeats and then over models. Timing repeats reduce timer noise for a fixed model and are NOT independent statistical replications. (d) serialized model size counting float64 parameters (one weight, one mean and one variance per component in one dimension). Fitting cost and query cost are reported separately because they trade off in opposite directions: the kernel estimators fit almost instantly but carry the whole sample at query time, whereas the dictionary methods pay a fitting cost once and then evaluate in time independent of N.

\section{A repaired estimator}
\label{sec:repaired}

The diagnoses of Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:bandwidth} point at
specific causes: a weighting matrix that is not the metric the error is
scored in, a first stage that does not converge, a penalty grid whose lower
end produces catastrophic fits, and a dictionary whose scales are anchored to
a rule-of-thumb bandwidth and whose widest atoms are never used. This section
implements the corresponding repairs as switches of one estimator, selects a
configuration on development seeds by a rule fixed in advance, and confirms
it on the confirmation seeds of Section~\ref{sec:setup} against the saved
fits of every comparator. Every number below is generated from the saved
rows (\texttt{followup/improved/}); the protocol was locked before the
development run. It is a prospective protocol, not a preregistration.

\subsection{Repairs}

\paragraph{Metric-consistent weighting.} With matched readings, Proposition~3
shows that $\Omega = G^{-1}$ turns the moment criterion into the
integrated-square loss. We therefore replace the inverse reading covariance
by $\Omega = (G + \epsilon I)^{-1}$, $\epsilon = 10^{-8}\,\overline{G_{ii}}$,
which removes $\rho$ and its grid. Whitening with the Cholesky factor of
$G$ gives $\|Au - b\|^2$ with $A = L^{-1}G$, $b = L^{-1}s$, so the first
stage is a non-negative lasso in residual space.

\paragraph{A converged first stage.} Stage one is solved by FISTA with
adaptive restart to a relative KKT residual of $10^{-8}$ (at most $20{,}000$
iterations), replacing the $500$-sweep coordinate descent; the achieved
residual is recorded on every fit.

\paragraph{No selection stage at all.} Because the simplex constraint already
yields sparse solutions, we also fit the integrated-square loss
$\tfrac12 w'Gw - s'w$ directly over the probability simplex on the full
dictionary by fully corrective Frank--Wolfe (each round adds the most
violating atom and re-solves exactly on the working set). This variant has
no $\lambda$, no $\rho$ and no $\tau$.

\paragraph{Scale-aware penalty, extended grid, one-SE rule.} For the
two-stage variants we tried a per-atom penalty $\lambda_i \propto
\sqrt{G_{ii}}$ (narrow atoms, with larger $L_2$ norm, must earn their place);
an eight-point $\lambda$ path extended to $3\times10^{-5}$ with the one-SE
rule (sparsest $\lambda$ within one standard error of the best validation
score) and five folds.

\paragraph{CV-anchored scales.} Instead of $\{0.05,\dots,4\}\times
h_{\mathrm{Silverman}}$, a base bandwidth $h_{\mathrm{cv}}$ is chosen by the
same held-out $J$ score from nine candidates on $[0.05h, 4h]$ and the
dictionary offers $\{\tfrac14, \tfrac12, 1, 2, 4\}\times h_{\mathrm{cv}}$ at
the same $60$ centres. This adds one tuned hyperparameter and is what the
single-scale ablation of Section~\ref{sec:bandwidth} was already doing.

\paragraph{Post-fit merge.} Near-duplicate atoms are greedily replaced by
their moment-matched Gaussian while the \emph{training} loss rises by less
than $1\%$, followed by a simplex refit of the weights; the truth is never
consulted.

\subsection{Development ablation and selection}

Ten variants were run on the development seeds of Section~\ref{sec:setup}
(four Marron--Wand targets, $N\in\{500,5000\}$, five datasets) and on two
targets of the heterogeneity family of Section~\ref{sec:bandwidth}, paired
with the confirmed finalist AKDE-M on identical data and folds
(Table~\ref{tab:imp-dev}, Figure~\ref{fig:imp-dev}). The selection rule,
fixed in the locked protocol, was the equal-weight geometric-mean paired ISE
ratio against AKDE-M over the eight Marron--Wand development cells, subject
to three gates (ratio $\le 1$ on the heterogeneity cells; both stages at
tolerance in at least $90\%$ of fits; no errors), with ties within $10\%$
broken by fewer tuned hyperparameters and then lower fit time.

Three findings shaped the choice. First, the Gram-weighted two-stage
variant, the identity-weighted variant and the full-simplex fit all reach
ratios of $\irDevIOneGramFista$, $\irDevITwoIdentityFista$ and $\irDevC$ against AKDE-M,
and the full-simplex fit reproduces SPADES-simplex to solver precision: the
covariance weighting was costing $15$--$16\%$ and the Frank--Wolfe simplex
fit matches the two-stage lasso with no tuning at all. Second, anchoring the
dictionary scales to a validated bandwidth is by far the largest single
repair: $\irDevR$ $[\irDevLoR, \irDevHiR]$ for the full-simplex fit with
CV-anchored scales. The scale-aware penalty reaches a similar ratio
($\irDevIFiveGramColnorm$) at nine times the cost, and combining both
reaches $\irDevIOneZeroGramColnormCvanchor$ but fails the convergence gate
and costs $\irDevWallIOneZeroGramColnormCvanchor$~s per fit. Third, the
extended-grid/one-SE rule ($\irDevISixGramExtOneseFivef$) and the merge step
($\irDevIEightSimplexMerge$ alone, $\irDevINineSimplexCvanchorMerge$ with
anchoring) do not help accuracy; the merge cuts the component count by half or more at a
measurable accuracy cost and is kept as an optional post-processing step,
not as part of the estimator. The locked rule selects the full-simplex fit
with CV-anchored scales, called \textbf{AKDE-R} below, and its
parameter-free core (the same fit on the Silverman-anchored dictionary,
\textbf{AKDE-R core}) as the ablation to confirm.

\subsection{Confirmation}

AKDE-R and its core were fitted to the $1{,}350$ confirmation datasets of
Section~\ref{sec:setup} ($15$ targets, $N\in\{200,1000,5000\}$, $R=30$) and
compared with the saved Stage-C fits of AKDE-M, AKDE-V2, SPADES-simplex, the
Sheather--Jones and Abramson estimators and the EM mixture on the identical
observations. All $2{,}700$ new fits completed, all are valid densities, and
the second-stage KKT residual is below $10^{-7}$ in every fit (the
Frank--Wolfe solve has no first stage). Table~\ref{tab:imp-bench} gives the
benchmark mean $\pm$ pooled SD in the format of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_sd},
Table~\ref{tab:imp-ratio} the fixed-benchmark paired ratios, and
Figure~\ref{fig:imp-acc} the per-target picture.

\paragraph{Against AKDE-M.} AKDE-R attains $\exp(T) = \irExpTRVsMNSmall$
$[\irExpTLoRVsMNSmall, \irExpTHiRVsMNSmall]$, $\irExpTRVsMNMid$
$[\irExpTLoRVsMNMid, \irExpTHiRVsMNMid]$ and $\irExpTRVsMNLarge$
$[\irExpTLoRVsMNLarge, \irExpTHiRVsMNLarge]$ against AKDE-M at
$N=200$, $1000$, $5000$, improving $\irFavRVsMNSmall$, $\irFavRVsMNMid$
and $\irFavRVsMNLarge$ of the $15$ targets. The benchmark mean ISE falls
from $\irPooledMNSmall$, $\irPooledMNMid$, $\irPooledMNLarge$ to
$\irPooledRNSmall$, $\irPooledRNMid$, $\irPooledRNLarge$. The core
alone, which differs from AKDE-M only in the criterion and the solver,
accounts for $\irExpTCVsMNSmall$, $\irExpTCVsMNMid$, $\irExpTCVsMNLarge$
of that; the rest is the anchoring of the scales.

\paragraph{Against SPADES-simplex and the kernel estimators.} The core is
indistinguishable from SPADES-simplex ($\exp(T) = \irExpTCVsSpadesNSmall$,
$\irExpTCVsSpadesNMid$, $\irExpTCVsSpadesNLarge$), as Proposition~3
predicts, so the identity is now an empirical fact on $1{,}350$ datasets.
AKDE-R is ahead of SPADES-simplex by $\irExpTRVsSpadesNSmall$,
$\irExpTRVsSpadesNMid$ and $\irExpTRVsSpadesNLarge$ on the same
$60$ centres, which is the gain from choosing the scales by validation; a
SPADES adaptation given the same anchored dictionary would be expected to
match it. Against the Sheather--Jones estimator the ratio is
$\irExpTRVsSjNSmall$ $[\irExpTLoRVsSjNSmall, \irExpTHiRVsSjNSmall]$,
$\irExpTRVsSjNMid$ and $\irExpTRVsSjNLarge$, with the intervals
excluding one: the repaired estimator remains about $30\%$ behind the kernel
estimator in paired geometric-mean ISE, down from the $2.6$--$4.9\times$ of
Section~\ref{sec:results}, and it is better than the kernel estimator on
$\irFavRVsSjNSmall$, $\irFavRVsSjNMid$ and $\irFavRVsSjNLarge$ of the
$15$ targets. It is behind the Abramson estimator by
$\irExpTRVsAbrNSmall$--$\irExpTRVsAbrNLarge$ and the EM mixture by
$\irExpTRVsGmmNSmall$--$\irExpTRVsGmmNLarge$.

\paragraph{Reliability.} AKDE-R produced $\irLargeR$ large-error fits
($\irLargeFracR\%$, worst $\irWorstR\times$ its cell median) against
$\irLargeM$ for AKDE-M (worst $\irWorstM\times$); the core, like
SPADES-simplex, produced none. The large-error mechanism of
Section~\ref{sec:results} (the lower end of the $\lambda$ grid) no longer
exists because there is no $\lambda$; all seven remaining cases occur at
$N=200$ and none of them selected a base bandwidth at the edge of its grid.

\subsection{Compactness and cost}

Table~\ref{tab:imp-cost} and Figure~\ref{fig:imp-cost} give the operating
points. AKDE-R stores $\irCompRNLarge$ components on average at $N=5000$
against $\irCompMNLarge$ for AKDE-M, and its query cost
($\irQueryRNLarge\,\mu$s per point) remains two orders of magnitude below
the kernel estimators. Its fitting cost ($\irWallRNLarge$~s at $N=5000$
including the nine-candidate bandwidth search) is comparable to AKDE-M's
($\irWallMNLarge$~s); the core fits in $\irWallCNLarge$~s because it has
nothing to tune. With the optional merge step the component count halves
again at a measurable accuracy cost (development evidence,
Table~\ref{tab:imp-dev}).

\subsection{The heterogeneity family revisited}

On the family $f_r$ of Section~\ref{sec:bandwidth} (same $160$ datasets,
comparator rows reused), AKDE-R improves on AKDE-M by paired factors of
$\irHrRVsMROneNFiveH$ ($r=1$) to $\irHrRVsMREightNFiveH$ ($r=8$) at $N=500$ and
$\irHrRVsMROneNLarge$ to $\irHrRVsMREightNLarge$ at $N=5000$
(Table~\ref{tab:imp-hr}, Figure~\ref{fig:imp-hr}). Against the single-scale
ablation of that section, which already used a validated bandwidth, it is
within a factor of $2.6$ in every cell and better in three of eight; the
multiscale dictionary, once anchored, is neither clearly better nor clearly
worse than one validated bandwidth on this family. Against the
Sheather--Jones estimator the ratios are $\irHrRVsSjRFourNLarge$ and
$\irHrRVsSjREightNLarge$ at $r=4,8$, $N=5000$, and $\irHrRVsSjROneNFiveH$
at $r=1$, $N=500$: the kernel estimator still wins where the density is
smooth and the sample small.

\subsection{What the repairs establish, and what they do not}

The repaired estimator keeps everything the original one was valued for: a
convex fit, a valid density by construction, a few dozen atoms and
microsecond queries. It removes the three numerical pathologies documented
earlier (a non-converging first stage, a weighting that is not the scoring
metric, a penalty grid edge that produces catastrophic fits) and replaces
three tuned hyperparameters by one. Its accuracy is $2$--$4\times$ better than
AKDE-M's and $1.7$--$3\times$ better than the SPADES adaptation's on the
original dictionary, and the gap to a Sheather--Jones kernel estimator is now
about $30\%$ rather than several fold. It is still behind every conventional
comparator on paired geometric-mean ISE; the compactness advantage, not
accuracy, remains the reason to use it. The gain is almost entirely
attributable to choosing the dictionary scales by validation, which any
dictionary method including SPADES can do; the moment-matching framing
contributed the diagnosis, not the gain. Two limitations are specific to the repaired estimator. The validated base
bandwidth sits at the top of its nine-point grid ($4h_{\mathrm{Silverman}}$)
in $57\%$ of the confirmation fits, so the anchor grid is too narrow at the
wide end and a wider grid may improve it further; it was not widened because
the protocol was locked. And development and confirmation used disjoint
seeds with a selection rule fixed in advance, but the repairs themselves were
chosen with the earlier results in hand, so this is a confirmed follow-up,
not an independent replication.
